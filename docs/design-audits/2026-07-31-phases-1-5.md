# Design Audit · 2026-07-31 (after phases 1 to 5)

## Corrections, 2026-07-31

An independent review of this report found six factual errors in it. They are
corrected in place below, and listed here because a reader who saw the first
version needs to know what changed rather than having to diff it.

1. **The report contradicted its own scorecard.** It said "Every criterion is at
   or above 9.2" directly beneath a table showing criteria 1 and 2 at 9.0. The
   table was right and the sentence was wrong.
2. **The spacing fix did not do what this report said it did.** `auto-fit` does
   not stretch an incomplete final row. A three or five item band at phone width
   still left an empty cell. The claim was verified at 1440px only, where five
   tracks happen to fit, and generalized from there.
3. **Summary values were truncated.** Every value carried `truncate`, so a large
   asset quantity clipped mid-number rather than wrapping.
4. **The ledger equation printed `=` between unequal values** when inputs were
   unresolved, asserting an equality the page's own caption denied.
5. **`scripts/capture.mjs` is not in this repository.** It lives in the tooling
   that produced the captures, so the instruction to point it at a competitor
   was not followable by a reader of this repo.
6. **The accessibility claim was overstated.** It read "zero axe violations on
   thirteen routes in both themes". The suite covered eleven routes, and exactly
   one of them in the light theme.

The scorecard is unchanged by these corrections. Defects 2, 3, 4 and 6 were then
fixed; see "Gate 0" at the end.

## Verdict

The explorer now answers its questions rather than displaying its data: every
record leads with a plain-English outcome, the overview opens with operational
health instead of two all-time totals, and native assets have identity, pages
and a roster where there was hex. The biggest win since the post-remediation
audit is that the two weakest capabilities in the competitive scorecard,
analytics at 4.8 and native assets at 5.8, are no longer absent. The biggest
remaining risk is that all of this has been judged against a fixture rather
than a running node, and against my estimates of competitors rather than
captures of them.

## Method and its limits

Captured at 1440×900 and 390×844, in both themes, across thirteen routes, from
a production build served against the fixture backend. Scores are mine against
the same ten-criterion rubric as the previous two audits, so they are
comparable with each other and are not measurements of anything else.

Two things in this report are evidence rather than opinion, and they are the
parts worth trusting:

- the layout table, which comes from `e2e/layout.spec.ts` running under
  `LAYOUT_MATRIX=full LAYOUT_GATES=enforce`;
- the defect list, every entry of which was found by a check rather than by
  looking.

## What changed

**Phase 1.** One journey grammar across transactions, blocks and list rows. The
lifecycle stepper, admission timeline and settlement band are deleted, not
merely hidden; their invariants moved onto the component that renders now. List
rows gained a four-dot indicator whose step ordering is shared with the full
journey, so a row and the record it links to cannot disagree.

**Phase 2.** An operational panel: chain tip and its age judged against the
observed block cadence rather than a fixed threshold, throughput, admission and
settlement percentiles, finality backlog, hourly production, and the oldest
block still waiting on L1. Every figure names the node column behind it, every
percentile carries its sample, and a sample under twenty records is labelled
thin rather than presented as a measurement.

**Phase 3.** Native assets: names decoded only when they round-trip to their own
bytes and carry nothing that can reorder text, CIP-14 fingerprints checked
against all eight specification vectors, quantities formatted as strings, an
asset page and a ledger-wide roster. The address page became four tabs and now
lists the individual UTxOs behind its balance.

**Phase 4.** The ledger equation, inputs = outputs + fee, stated only when every
input resolved, with per-address net movement under the same rule.

**Phase 5.** Search offers ranked candidates for ambiguous input instead of
guessing, resolves CIP-14 fingerprints, and looks up partial hashes of six
characters or more. Blocks and transactions gained a settlement filter that
runs in SQL before pagination, with CSV and JSON export and URL-persisted state.

## Defects found by checks, not by looking

1. **Fixture status cycles were phase-locked.** Transaction statuses and
   finalization statuses both cycled mod 7 against a height decrementing in
   step, so every committed transaction landed in an abandoned block and
   "committed, awaiting L1 finality" could not be produced at all. A test that
   had been green was passing against a stale fixture server.
2. **Fixture amounts never balanced.** Inputs never equalled outputs plus fee,
   so the case every genuine transaction satisfies could not be rendered.
3. **JSX dropped spaces between elements and following text.** "1 of 2inputs",
   "Receivedis exact", "Spentappears only" all shipped. Found by scanning the
   rendered text of every route, not by reading the screenshots, which is how
   they survived a visual pass.
4. **The list tools bar overflowed at 320px**, caught by the overflow gate.
5. **The a11y sweep looped nine routes inside one test**, sharing one timeout
   and failing without naming the page at fault. Now one test per route.
6. **An unrecognized settlement status reported an `active` outcome** (phase 1,
   caught by a journey invariant test).
7. **Four of five CIP-14 vectors I started from were wrong**, recalled rather
   than transcribed. The implementation was correct; the test was not.

## Closing the last four

The first pass of this audit scored spacing, typography, rhythm and motion at
8.5 and named what each needed. All four were then done.

- **Spacing.** Summary bands fixed their column count per breakpoint and painted
  invisible filler cells over the remainder, so a five-item band on the block
  page rendered a four-column grid with three-quarters of a row empty. Tracks
  were changed to size themselves with `auto-fit`, which removed the filler
  machinery. This was verified at 1440px only, and the report then claimed the
  final row stretched to fill, which `auto-fit` does not do. See correction 2:
  the phone-width case was still broken and was fixed in Gate 0.
- **Typography.** The small band had drifted to 12px, 12.5px and 13px, three
  tiers a reader cannot tell apart and a writer cannot choose between. Two named
  tokens replace them, `mg-caption` for secondary prose and `mg-micro` for
  provenance, declared in the components layer so a `leading-*` utility
  alongside either still wins. Sixty-one raw size classes became two names.
- **Rhythm.** The assets roster stopped mid-page when the ledger held few
  assets. It now ends on a three-part explanation of how to read an asset,
  which is worth the space at any list length rather than being filler.
- **Motion.** There was no motion that carried meaning. The three figures that
  actually move as a node produces blocks now tint once when their value
  changes, never on mount, so arriving at the page does not look like a burst of
  activity that did not happen. The global reduced-motion rule collapses it.

None of these was a structural problem, which is why they were left until the
five phases of substance were finished.

## What this audit cannot tell you

Two of the release gates in the strategy this work followed need input this
audit does not have:

- **Task performance against real users.** No user has been observed using any
  of this.
- **Competitor position.** The comparison scores in the scorecard this work was
  aimed at are calibrated estimates from training knowledge, not captures. No
  competitor has been put through this matrix. Doing so needs a capture harness
  that takes an arbitrary base URL and runs the viewport by theme matrix over a
  route list; that harness is not in this repository, which is why the first
  version of this report pointed at a path (`scripts/capture.mjs`) that a reader
  here cannot run. Until a competitor is actually captured, no claim about
  ranking against one belongs in a report.

Everything else here was verified: the unit suite, the end-to-end suite across
desktop and mobile projects, axe clean on every audited route, and the layout
gates enforced across the full matrix of three lifecycle states at four widths.
Exact counts are deliberately not quoted here, because they change with every
commit and a stale number in a report reads as a measurement when it is not.

## Gate 0

The defects an independent review found, and what closed each. The scores above
are unchanged: none of this adds capability, it removes reasons to distrust the
report. What is worth noting is that four of the six were things this report
asserted were already true.

**The suite was not a gate.** `FIXTURE` was hard-coded to port 3101 while
`playwright.config.ts` read `FIXTURE_PORT`, so setting that variable started a
fixture the tests never talked to. Worse, `reuseExistingServer` adopts whatever
is already listening, `populated.spec.ts` never reset fault injection, and
`core-flows.spec.ts` reset only in `afterEach`, so a run interrupted while
`fail=all` was injected left a server that failed every request for the next
run. That is the mechanism behind a reviewer seeing failures that did not
reproduce here. Now: the port is derived from the same variable, a global setup
proves the process on it is this fixture and refuses to run otherwise, and an
auto-fixture clears injected faults before and after every test. Verified by
poisoning a fixture with `fail=all` and running the whole suite against it
twice: 200 passed both times, the same result as from a clean start.

**Summary bands left an empty cell at phone width.** `auto-fit` collapses empty
tracks but does not stretch the ones it keeps, so a three or five item band left
a cell of exposed border colour that reads as a metric that failed to load. Now
flex-wrapped with growing cells, which is the behaviour the grid was being asked
for. The gate that proves it reads geometry at four widths and asserts every row
reaches its container's right edge. Written for the summary band, it immediately
found the same defect a second time in the journey stage list, which nobody had
noticed.

**Values were truncated.** Every summary value carried `truncate`, so a large
quantity clipped mid-number. A clipped number is not an abbreviated number, it
is a different number. Values now wrap. This also caught a case nobody had
reported: `relativeTime` falls back to a full absolute timestamp past thirty
days, and `Timestamp` sets `whitespace-nowrap`, so on real data an old address's
first-activity date would clip permanently.

**The ledger equation stated a falsehood.** With unresolved inputs it rendered
"At least ₳9.501 = ₳4.7 + ₳0.171", asserting an equality between a lower bound
and an exact sum, in the one component whose entire purpose is to be true. The
identity still holds, so the left term now says it is not known in full, with
the resolved portion beneath it as context rather than inside the equation.

**Not-found pages answered HTTP 200.** A root `loading.tsx` wrapped every route
in a Suspense boundary, so Next flushed a 200 shell before any page body ran and
a later `notFound()` could not change the status. Removed; list routes keep
their own `loading.tsx`, detail routes must not have one. Reasoning recorded in
`app/src/app/LOADING.md`, and all five status codes are now asserted directly.

**The accessibility claim was backwards.** The theme follows
`prefers-color-scheme` and Playwright emulates light unless told otherwise, so
every route was audited in light and none in dark, while one extra test asked
for light a second time. Dark had never been audited at all. Now every route
runs both, and the helper fails if the two passes render the same background, so
the claim cannot be made falsely again. Dark was clean on first run.

**Prettier was red on 21 files**, in a repository whose own `ci-local.sh` runs it.
Formatted. Because reflowing JSX is exactly what creates the dropped-space
defect this project has shipped before, the rendered-text scan that catches it
is now `e2e/prose.spec.ts` rather than a technique to remember.

One unit test deserves its own line: `SummaryBand` had a test asserting the
class name `auto-fit`, and it passed for the entire period the band was broken.
A test that asserts the mechanism cannot fail when the mechanism is the bug. It
now asserts the property, and the real proof is the measured gate.
