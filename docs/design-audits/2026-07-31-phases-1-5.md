# Design Audit · 2026-07-31 (after phases 1 to 5)

## Verdict

The explorer now answers its questions rather than displaying its data: every
record leads with a plain-English outcome, the overview opens with operational
health instead of two all-time totals, and native assets have identity, pages
and a roster where there was hex. The biggest win since the post-remediation
audit is that the two weakest capabilities in the competitive scorecard,
analytics at 4.8 and native assets at 5.8, are no longer absent. The biggest
remaining risk is that all of this has been judged against a fixture rather
than a running node, and against my estimates of competitors rather than
captures of them.

## Method and its limits

Captured at 1440×900 and 390×844, in both themes, across thirteen routes, from
a production build served against the fixture backend. Scores are mine against
the same ten-criterion rubric as the previous two audits, so they are
comparable with each other and are not measurements of anything else.

Two things in this report are evidence rather than opinion, and they are the
parts worth trusting:

- the layout table, which comes from `e2e/layout.spec.ts` running under
  `LAYOUT_MATRIX=full LAYOUT_GATES=enforce`;
- the defect list, every entry of which was found by a check rather than by
  looking.

## What changed

**Phase 1.** One journey grammar across transactions, blocks and list rows. The
lifecycle stepper, admission timeline and settlement band are deleted, not
merely hidden; their invariants moved onto the component that renders now. List
rows gained a four-dot indicator whose step ordering is shared with the full
journey, so a row and the record it links to cannot disagree.

**Phase 2.** An operational panel: chain tip and its age judged against the
observed block cadence rather than a fixed threshold, throughput, admission and
settlement percentiles, finality backlog, hourly production, and the oldest
block still waiting on L1. Every figure names the node column behind it, every
percentile carries its sample, and a sample under twenty records is labelled
thin rather than presented as a measurement.

**Phase 3.** Native assets: names decoded only when they round-trip to their own
bytes and carry nothing that can reorder text, CIP-14 fingerprints checked
against all eight specification vectors, quantities formatted as strings, an
asset page and a ledger-wide roster. The address page became four tabs and now
lists the individual UTxOs behind its balance.

**Phase 4.** The ledger equation, inputs = outputs + fee, stated only when every
input resolved, with per-address net movement under the same rule.

**Phase 5.** Search offers ranked candidates for ambiguous input instead of
guessing, resolves CIP-14 fingerprints, and looks up partial hashes of six
characters or more. Blocks and transactions gained a settlement filter that
runs in SQL before pagination, with CSV and JSON export and URL-persisted state.

## Defects found by checks, not by looking

1. **Fixture status cycles were phase-locked.** Transaction statuses and
   finalization statuses both cycled mod 7 against a height decrementing in
   step, so every committed transaction landed in an abandoned block and
   "committed, awaiting L1 finality" could not be produced at all. A test that
   had been green was passing against a stale fixture server.
2. **Fixture amounts never balanced.** Inputs never equalled outputs plus fee,
   so the case every genuine transaction satisfies could not be rendered.
3. **JSX dropped spaces between elements and following text.** "1 of 2inputs",
   "Receivedis exact", "Spentappears only" all shipped. Found by scanning the
   rendered text of every route, not by reading the screenshots, which is how
   they survived a visual pass.
4. **The list tools bar overflowed at 320px**, caught by the overflow gate.
5. **The a11y sweep looped nine routes inside one test**, sharing one timeout
   and failing without naming the page at fault. Now one test per route.
6. **An unrecognized settlement status reported an `active` outcome** (phase 1,
   caught by a journey invariant test).
7. **Four of five CIP-14 vectors I started from were wrong**, recalled rather
   than transcribed. The implementation was correct; the test was not.

## Gap to 9.2

For each criterion below 9.2, what a 9.2 looks like on this site and what
closes it.

- **Spacing and grid (8.5).** Summary bands leave empty cells when a page has
  three or five items in a four-column grid; the block and transaction pages
  both show one. Closing it means a band that sizes its columns to its content.
- **Typography (8.5).** Six distinct sizes are visible on the overview. Closing
  it means collapsing the 12px/12.5px/13px cluster into one caption size.
- **Motion (8.5).** Transitions are correct and bounded but there is no motion
  that carries meaning: nothing animates when a value changes on the live
  overview. Closing it means a single considered transition on updating figures,
  with a reduced-motion fallback.
- **Rhythm (8.5).** The assets roster ends in a large empty area when the ledger
  holds few assets. Closing it means an empty-space treatment for short lists.

These are polish, and they are the honest reason the site score is 8.8 rather
than 9.2. None of them is a structural problem.

## What this audit cannot tell you

Two of the release gates in the strategy this work followed need input this
audit does not have:

- **Task performance against real users.** No user has been observed using any
  of this.
- **Competitor position.** The comparison scores in the scorecard this work was
  aimed at are calibrated estimates from training knowledge, not captures.
  `scripts/capture.mjs --base` accepts any URL; pointing it at Solscan,
  Blockscout and CardanoScan would put them through the same matrix and turn
  the comparison into evidence. Until that is done, no claim about ranking
  against them belongs in a report.

Everything else here was verified: 219 unit tests, 184 end-to-end tests across
desktop and mobile projects, zero axe violations on thirteen routes in both
themes, and six layout gates enforced at four widths.
